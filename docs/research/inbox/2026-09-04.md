# AlphaOS weekly research digest — 2026-09-04

## A temporally coherent benchmark is a versioned snapshot, not a fixed prompt with live context
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08598 — arXiv / University of Chicago, 2025-10-31
- **Type:** paper
- **Bears on:** priority question 1
- **What it establishes:** OKBench treats the complete dated knowledge environment as the benchmark unit. Each release carries a signature covering the generator model and revision, decoding settings, generation timestamp, and a unique identifier; reported comparisons point to one preserved daily snapshot. In that framing, appending current bars to July packets is a dataset-version change, while either stored July bars or a deterministic reconstruction at a pinned July as-of time can form a coherent snapshot. A later re-freeze defines a new benchmark population rather than an in-place repair of the old one.
- **Evidence basis:** The main experiment used a single 2025-03-22 snapshot containing 2,350 questions generated from the preceding 24 hours of news. Four annotators assessed 200 questions and produced a 92% average clarity pass rate; multiple open and proprietary models were then compared on that fixed snapshot with no context, oracle context, and retrieval over 1-, 5-, and 10-day corpora. This was a fixed-benchmark experiment, not a walk-forward or live production study.
- **Limitations:** News question answering is not trade evaluation, and the paper does not test internally contradictory financial inputs or methods for bridging two frozen corpus versions. Its randomly generated MD5 identifier names a release but does not by itself prove content identity. The automated question-generation and validation stages also introduce evaluator noise absent from a hand-curated 60-packet corpus.
- **Implementation tradeoffs:** Byte-level preservation of packets, bars, prompts, tool outputs, model identifier, and evaluator settings adds a manifest and more retained artifacts but is modest at 60 packets. Deterministic as-of reconstruction stores less market data but inherits vendor corrections, corporate-action logic, calendar handling, and API reproducibility as new failure surfaces. A re-freeze preserves temporal validity at the cost of breaking direct score comparability; a crossed bridge panel that runs both evaluator versions on both corpus versions adds calls and separates corpus drift from evaluator drift only over the overlapping cases.
- **What would have to be true on AlphaOS's own data for this to matter:** Materiality would appear as a large paired reduction in staleness rejections when the same evaluator sees a temporally coherent packet-plus-bars snapshot, without a corresponding collapse in agreement on the packets that were already coherent. With 60 paired packets, rejection-rate changes are measurable; attribution across a re-freeze requires overlap or a crossed old/new-corpus by old/new-evaluator comparison, because an unbridged score difference confounds both changes.

## Few-cluster inference depends more on the dependence partition and leverage than on raw replay count
- **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2022.04.001 — *Journal of Econometrics* 232(2), 2023-02
- **Type:** paper
- **Bears on:** priority question 2
- **What it establishes:** Cluster-robust inference permits arbitrary dependence within a cluster but assumes independence across clusters, so the unit follows the plausible data-generating dependence rather than the most convenient label. The paper finds ordinary cluster-robust asymptotics unreliable with few or heterogeneous clusters and gives greater weight to CV3, restricted wild-cluster bootstrap, leverage and influence diagnostics, and the effective rather than nominal cluster count. It also warns that even those methods can fail when only a few clusters carry the identifying variation.
- **Evidence basis:** This is a peer-reviewed synthesis of econometric theory and prior simulation evidence, with an empirical reanalysis of minimum-wage effects on teenage labor supply and released replication files. It is neither a securities study nor an in-sample/out-of-sample trading experiment.
- **Limitations:** The primary setting is linear regression with disjoint, known clusters. Same-symbol holding intervals can overlap transitively, while common decision-day shocks create a second dependence dimension; neither partition is selected automatically by the methods. Roughly 20 nominal clusters is close to the range in which influence, imbalance, and a small effective cluster count can dominate nominal confidence coverage.
- **Implementation tradeoffs:** Symbol clustering can absorb repeated-symbol overlap but may leave very few large clusters; decision-day clustering captures shared market shocks but misses return increments shared by the same symbol across days. Multiway clustering or interval-connected components represents both mechanisms more honestly but can make the effective count smaller still. CV3 and wild-cluster calculations are computationally cheap at this scale, although pre-registering plausible partitions, bootstrap weights, confidence construction, and a conservative sensitivity envelope adds research-state complexity.
- **What would have to be true on AlphaOS's own data for this to matter:** Same-symbol overlapping outcomes or same-day decisions would need materially positive residual covariance, and no one or two clusters could supply most of the estimated effect. A lower performance floor has statistical meaning only if its one-sided CV3 or restricted-wild-bootstrap bound remains on the same side of the floor across defensible partitions and leave-one-cluster-out runs; if the effective cluster count collapses or the bound changes qualitatively, approximately 20 clusters cannot support a calibrated floor.

## Live retail executions show that a fixed one-basis-point cost is not broker-invariant
- **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13467 — *The Journal of Finance*, 2025-07-25
- **Type:** paper
- **Bears on:** priority question 3
- **What it establishes:** A funded trading experiment found economically meaningful execution-price variation for identical small US equity market orders sent nearly simultaneously through different retail brokerage accounts. Mean account-level round-trip cost ranged from 7 to 46 basis points, excluding commissions, even though most flow executed off exchange. The measurement used execution price relative to contemporaneous quote midpoints, demonstrating that execution cost is an observed broker-and-order outcome rather than a universal constant.
- **Evidence basis:** The authors placed 85,417 live trades across six accounts at five brokers from 2021-12-21 through 2022-06-09, covering 128 stocks and 113 trading days; the filtered analysis contained 74,801 trades. Target notional was mainly about $100, with robustness experiments around $1,000 and up to $5,000. This was live, funded execution evidence rather than a backtest.
- **Limitations:** The experiment used ordinary intraday market orders, not triggered stops, overnight gaps, or forced liquidations. Its 2021–2022 routing environment and named broker accounts need not match a current paper-broker path. The reported cross-broker average says little about the far-left tail of a stop fill, and the very large sample does not transfer its precision to a system with about one closed trade per week.
- **Implementation tradeoffs:** Comparable implementation-shortfall measurement adds order-submission, acknowledgement, execution, side, quantity, and contemporaneous quote records plus a definition of the reference price. A free IEX-tier feed is not a consolidated NBBO/TAQ source, so exact replication of the paper's benchmark would add data cost; an IEX-only or last-bar proxy leaves venue and timestamp basis error inside the residual. Broker-specific calibration also creates drift whenever routing or account handling changes.
- **What would have to be true on AlphaOS's own data for this to matter:** Per-order realized shortfall relative to a pre-registered arrival-price proxy would need to exceed one basis point per side consistently, or vary predictably with spread, volatility, order type, or broker state. At roughly 20 independent clusters, a rich impact curve is not identifiable, but paired replay-versus-realized residuals can still reveal whether a constant-cost model is directionally and economically miscalibrated.

## Stop-market protection exchanges price certainty for execution certainty
- **Source:** https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/stop-orders-factors-consider-during-volatile-markets — FINRA, 2025-03-26
- **Type:** report
- **Bears on:** priority question 3
- **What it establishes:** FINRA describes a stop price as a trigger, not a guaranteed fill: after election, a stop order becomes a market order and can execute materially away from the stop during rapid price movement. A stop-limit constrains the execution price but introduces non-execution risk. This separates gap-through-stop loss from ordinary spread or routing slippage and explains why an exact −1.0R stop assumption has no mechanical guarantee.
- **Evidence basis:** This is regulatory investor guidance describing US equity order mechanics and failure modes; it contains worked explanations but no sample, historical period, or empirical estimate. It is not in-sample, walk-forward, or live comparative evidence.
- **Limitations:** The source quantifies neither typical nor tail slippage and does not distinguish broker-side synthetic stops from venue-native handling. It offers no calibration for retail-size orders, opening gaps, halts, or securities admitted under a 1% spread gate.
- **Implementation tradeoffs:** Separating close-to-open or last-pre-trigger gap, trigger-to-arrival movement, and arrival-to-fill shortfall adds event timestamps, trigger-reference data, fill VWAP, and explicit missing-data states. Daily bars can identify many overnight gaps but cannot reconstruct intraday trigger sequencing, quote depth, or queue position. Stop-limit modeling adds a competing residual-exposure state rather than eliminating execution risk.
- **What would have to be true on AlphaOS's own data for this to matter:** The observable is the signed distribution of fill VWAP minus stop price, expressed both in basis points and R, with gap-to-trigger and trigger-to-fill components where timestamps permit. Repeated losses beyond −1.0R that concentrate in overnight gaps, wide spreads, or volatile names would make a conditional model relevant; a tail quantile is plainly not estimable from only a handful of stop exits or about 20 clusters, so any extreme-loss floor would remain a stress assumption rather than a statistically calibrated percentile.

## Kill-switch prior art records two legitimate but incompatible halt semantics
- **Source:** https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2015-12-17/pdf/2015-30533.pdf — US Commodity Futures Trading Commission, 2015-12-17
- **Type:** report
- **Bears on:** priority question 4
- **What it establishes:** The Regulation AT proposal records an explicit industry split. One design removes an entity from the market completely and uses separate order-management tools for any later risk reduction; another preserves manual or automatically classified risk-reducing orders while blocking risk-increasing activity. Commenters identified the central failure modes on both sides: a hard halt can prevent exposure reduction, while an exception inside the kill path adds uncertainty, classification error, and operational complexity.
- **Evidence basis:** The 125-page Federal Register proposal synthesizes comments from exchanges, clearing firms, trading firms, and industry groups on existing and proposed futures-market controls. It documents deployed control patterns and expert positions but does not report a controlled test, incident sample, or comparative failure rate.
- **Limitations:** The setting is institutional futures trading with exchange and clearing-member control layers, not a long-only retail equity account on one machine. The proposal is historical and does not prove that either semantic choice is safer for a particular broker API. It also does not resolve the special case in which cancelling a protective order increases risk before a liquidation fill completes.
- **Implementation tradeoffs:** A total halt has a small state space and a clear invariant but can strand positions or stale protective orders. A risk-reducing carve-out can shrink exposure during a fault, yet its safety depends on fresh positions, pending-fill awareness, direction and quantity validation, authorization, and a trusted execution path distinct from the failed strategy path. Separate cancel, liquidate, and re-protect phases create intermediate states that require durable reconciliation after restart.
- **What would have to be true on AlphaOS's own data for this to matter:** In fault-injection and replayed order-state races, every action admitted during a halt would need to weakly reduce worst-case exposure after pending fills are included, and any residual position would need continuous, quantity-matched protection or a bounded documented unprotected interval. Live incident frequency at about one trade per week is far too low for comparative failure-rate estimation; deterministic state-machine coverage and injected broker failures are the observable evidence available at this scale.

## Partial liquidation is an asynchronous order-state reconciliation problem
- **Source:** https://www.fixtrading.org/wp-content/uploads/download-manager-files/FIX-Latest-as-of-EP284-Order-State-Changes.pdf — FIX Trading Community, 2023-11
- **Type:** engineering write-up
- **Bears on:** priority question 5
- **What it establishes:** The FIX order-state matrices represent each execution with order quantity, cumulative filled quantity, last fill, and leaves quantity, and they explicitly model fills that cross cancel or replace requests in flight. A pending-cancel or pending-replace label can temporarily take precedence while additional executions still change cumulative quantity. The accepted pattern is therefore convergence from an execution ledger and current broker state, not treating a submitted cancel or close as an atomic terminal event.
- **Evidence basis:** This 57-page industry specification supplies worked state-transition sequences for new, partial, filled, cancelled, replaced, and race-condition cases, including fills arriving while cancel/replace is pending. The examples are normative and synthetic; there is no empirical dataset, period, walk-forward test, or live failure-rate estimate.
- **Limitations:** Retail REST and streaming APIs may omit FIX fields, reorder or duplicate notifications, and expose broker-specific parent/child semantics. The specification does not guarantee atomic protective-order resizing, prescribe a liquidation retry policy, or address a particular paper broker. Paper fills may also underrepresent live partial-fill and venue-race behavior.
- **Implementation tradeoffs:** An append-only fill ledger keyed by stable order and execution identifiers, idempotent event handling, broker-position snapshots, and protection quantity reconciliation add durable state and recovery logic. The additional paths include duplicate events, late fills after cancellation, stale position reads, rejected protection replacements, and accidental over-closing if a retry ignores cumulative fills. Polling can close streaming gaps but adds latency and rate-limit exposure.
- **What would have to be true on AlphaOS's own data for this to matter:** Injected sequences containing partial fills, late fills, cancel rejects, duplicate events, process restarts, and stale snapshots would need to converge to broker position equals internal position and protective leaves quantity equals residual shares within a measured time bound, without duplicate liquidation. Natural production frequency is too low to estimate the failure probability at one closed trade per week; replay coverage of every reachable transition and invariant violations is the testable evidence.
